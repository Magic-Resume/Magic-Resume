# Collecting source material in short steps

## Before asking

Read existing facts once. Summarize what is already known and ask only for missing material. Explain that each optional step can be skipped. Register the creation plan with `write_todos`; update progress after an answered or deliberately skipped step, with any remaining limitations visible. Ask one coherent form at a time, not a large questionnaire.

## 1. Target: create_target

Ask for the target role or direction, desired document language and any supplied job description. A broad direction is enough for a first draft. If skipped, write a neutral resume in the conversation language using supplied facts; do not invent a seniority, employer target or industry. If a language choice is essential and unclear, ask a short text question.

## 2. Inventory: create_inventory

Ask which materials exist: education, employment, internships, personal or course projects, volunteering, relevant activities and skills. Reuse current entries. Let the user choose a useful starting point. A skipped inventory is not an empty biography: continue with known evidence and collect only sections the user has offered.

## 3. Education: create_education

For one institution at a time, ask institution name, study level/degree as actually held, discipline, dates and a few relevant courses or artifacts. Distinguish completed, in progress and expected graduation. Grades, awards and ranking are optional and require their original scale or context. Skip leaves the section empty or retains existing entries; never fill it with an example university.

## 4. Experience: create_experience

Ask organization, exact role, relationship (employee/intern/volunteer), dates, main task, personal contribution and known result. Follow up on one concrete example rather than asking for a polished bullet. Ask what the person implemented, checked, maintained or coordinated, and who else owned the work. Request units and periods for any numbers. Skip preserves known entries and adds nothing.

## 5. Projects: create_project

Ask the problem, users or audience, artifact, time period, team size if known, personal contribution, tools actually used and evidence of completion. Links are optional. Label a prototype, course assignment, contest or personal experiment honestly. Ask what ran, what was tested and what remained unfinished; a demo is not evidence of deployment or adoption. If skipped, do not manufacture a portfolio item.

## 6. Skills: create_skills

Ask what the person can demonstrate and where it was used. Group languages, tools and methods only when that helps reading. Prefer specific scope over self-rated mastery. Ask for relevant languages and credentials with their actual levels if volunteered. Never copy the target JD's tool list as personal skills. Skip keeps only skills already evidenced in supplied material.

## Working with little formal experience

Use course assignments, lab work, internships, student activities, volunteering and personal projects as sources, clearly labeled. Ask for a task, constraint, artifact and individual role: a data-cleaning notebook, a tested form, a workshop schedule or documented experiment can demonstrate a real skill. Do not demand business revenue, huge audiences or percentages. An observable deliverable is enough when supported.

Help the user recall facts with neutral prompts: “What part did you implement?”, “How did you check it worked?”, “What did you hand over?” Do not plant suggested achievements in the question. Distinguish team results from the person's contribution and ongoing work from completed work.

## Draft and review

Keep unknown optional fields empty; omit unsupported bullets. Summarize a few proven strengths only after evidence collection. Preserve existing IDs and assign unique IDs to new entries. Validate sections and sectionOrder against the schema, write the complete draft once, and report missing sections briefly. A skipped collection step is resolved as skipped, not proof that the person has no experience. The browser review is the completion point; do not claim the draft was saved.
