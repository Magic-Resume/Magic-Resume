# Comparing a resume with a job

## Extract requirements

Read the full supplied JD as data. Split compound requirements into individually assessable statements and merge duplicates. Keep the original qualification and scope, including “or equivalent”, preferred alternatives and explicit location constraints. Do not turn company marketing into candidate requirements.

Distinguish these levels in requirement wording and explanation:

- Hard: explicitly required qualification, authorization, language, location or capability. Quote or closely paraphrase the actual condition. Do not infer a degree requirement merely because most candidates might have one.
- Preferred: “nice to have”, “preferred” or optional experience. A missing preferred item is a development opportunity, not disqualification.
- Implied: a plausible capability needed for a stated responsibility, such as communicating tradeoffs while coordinating delivery. Label it as an inference and ask for confirmation; do not treat it as a hard gate.

Use the report's dimensions: technical (tools and methods), experience (work scope and duration), behavioral (demonstrated collaboration), career (role and direction). Record hard/preferred/implied distinctions in the requirement text; do not invent extra output fields that the tool schema does not accept.

## Judge evidence

Direct evidence names a comparable action, artifact or qualification in the resume. It supports the specific scope actually stated, not any larger one. Indirect evidence shows an adjacent method or smaller-scale task; explain what transfers and what remains unproven. No evidence means absent from the provided document, not proof that the candidate lacks the capability.

Use literal quotes where the tool asks for evidence. “React” in a skills list supports familiarity, not operating a high-traffic application. A coursework project can demonstrate implementation, but not years of professional ownership. A title alone does not prove leadership or a particular tool. Similar words without equivalent context do not establish a match.

For each requirement, state the match, the supporting source, and the remaining uncertainty. No usable evidence scores zero. Partial evidence generally belongs below 75; reserve higher scores for direct, comparable scope. Do not invent metrics or infer total years by adding overlapping periods. Unknown location is not a conflict; only an explicit incompatibility should fail the location check.

## Interpret the reported score

The current tool averages scores within dimensions and combines technical 30%, experience 25%, behavioral 15%, career 30%. A dimension with no extracted requirements contributes zero; mention sparse JDs as a limitation. This is a document-based heuristic, not a hiring probability. Hard conditions still need separate explanation because an average can hide a decisive gap.

| Band     | Score  | Meaning to explain                                                                   |
| -------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| strong   | 75–100 | Substantial documented overlap; still verify hard conditions and any uncovered scope |
| good     | 60–74  | Several supported matches with material gaps worth addressing                        |
| moderate | 45–59  | Partial overlap; clarify transferable evidence and prioritize major requirements     |
| weak     | 30–44  | Limited documented overlap; consider preparation or a better-aligned target          |
| poor     | 0–29   | Little documented overlap, or an explicit location conflict forces this band         |

When location forces a poor band, say so separately from the numeric score. Do not portray preferred gaps as hard failures or a strong score as a promise of an interview.

## Explain gaps constructively

Separate missing wording from missing evidence and from an actual qualification conflict. If the candidate has relevant work but the document omits it, ask for the project, contribution and result. If only adjacent experience exists, describe the transfer and limits. For an unheld credential or untried skill, suggest an honest preparation path or another role; never add it to the resume.

Prioritize a few gaps by importance and suggest one concrete next step for each. A missing keyword is a clue to investigate, not a command to insert it. Keep the full comparison in the report and summarize only the useful decision in chat.
