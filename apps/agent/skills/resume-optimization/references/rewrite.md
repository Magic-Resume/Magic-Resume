# Evidence-led rewriting

## Sentence structure

Prefer action + scope + supported result: what the person did, for which system or audience, and what changed. Include the method when it explains the contribution. A result may be a verified deliverable or observable quality improvement; it need not be a percentage. Keep one main contribution per bullet. Distinguish “implemented”, “co-designed”, “supported” and “led” by actual ownership, not perceived seniority.

Avoid vague intensifiers such as outstanding, world-class or transformative. Replace a list of duties with a concrete contribution only when the source supports it. Preserve useful domain nouns and established technology names. Reordering evidence toward a JD is allowed; importing the JD's claims into the candidate's experience is not.

## Quantification

Keep each number with its unit, baseline, population and time period. “Median response time fell from 420 ms to 310 ms during the six-week trial” is stronger and more precise than “performance improved 26%”. If the source only gives 26%, keep it without inventing the start and end values. Do not turn percentage points into percentages, cumulative counts into monthly counts, estimates into audited facts, or a team result into an individual one.

Ask where a metric came from, what it measures, over which period and what the candidate owned. If the user cannot supply it, describe the deliverable without a number. Never offer an invented number as a proposed fact or leave a numeric placeholder in a finished resume. Approximate figures remain explicitly approximate. Preserve currencies, magnitudes, denominators and negative signs. Do not calculate a new rate when the comparison periods or populations differ.

## Chinese and English

Chinese bullets can start with 实现、设计、整理、排查、验证、协调、维护 or 协助 when supported. Keep subjects implicit where clear. Avoid oral fillers such as “然后”“搞定”“特别厉害”, vague “负责相关工作”, and unsupported “主导”“全面负责”. Concise phrases are acceptable if the action and object remain clear.

English bullets usually omit “I” and start with a specific verb. Use past tense for completed contributions; present tense may describe ongoing responsibilities. Match tense within an entry. Prefer “Implemented”, “Tested”, “Documented”, “Maintained” or “Contributed to” over “Responsible for”. Avoid translating Chinese noun chains word for word, unnecessary articles before technology names and grander titles than the source establishes.

## Section-specific checks

- Experience: retain employer, role and dates; name the person's contribution and distinguish collaboration from ownership. Keep causality modest when several changes affected an outcome.
- Projects: state the problem, the delivered artifact and individual role. Label coursework, prototypes and personal projects honestly. A demo is not a production deployment; a repository is not evidence of business adoption.
- Skills: list only explicitly supported skills with useful scope. Preserve proficiency honestly; using a tool once does not establish expertise. Group related tools without stuffing synonyms or JD keywords.
- Personal summary: synthesize two or three supported strengths and the target direction. Avoid new years-of-experience totals, credentials or self-ratings. Do not restate every experience item.

## Before and after examples

All people and organizations below are fictional. Examples teach editing decisions; none are facts about the user.

1. Chinese experience, clearer action. Alex Chen at Example Lantern Lab: “负责接口相关工作，做了一些检查。” → “维护订单查询接口，补充输入校验与异常处理。” This is valid only when the supplied notes identify those checks; otherwise ask what was checked.
2. English experience, preserved measurement. Avery Example at Example Harbor Tools: “I made the search faster, from 420 ms to 310 ms median over a six-week trial.” → “Optimized search queries, reducing median response time from 420 ms to 310 ms during a six-week trial.” No additional audience or scale is inferred.
3. Coursework project, honest scope. Rowan Example: “小组作业做了一个预约网页，我写了表单校验。” → “在课程小组的预约网页项目中实现表单校验。” Do not label coursework as paid employment.
4. Skills, supported organization. Mira Example: “用 Python 清洗 CSV，用 SQL 查数据。” → “数据处理：使用 Python 清洗 CSV，并通过 SQL 查询核对数据。” This does not imply advanced modeling or platform ownership.
5. Personal summary, evidence retained. Casey Example: “认真负责，有沟通能力。实习时维护过组件文档，也整理过测试记录。” → “有组件文档维护与测试记录整理的实习经验，注重信息表达与交付核对。” Do not add years or seniority.
6. DO NOT: inflated ownership. Drew Example at Example Orchard Studio: “协助团队上线支付页面，负责错误提示。” → BAD: “主导支付系统架构并独立交付全链路。” → Safer: “为支付页面上线补充错误提示，协助团队完成交付。” The system-wide ownership is absent from the source.
7. DO NOT: skills copied from a JD. Sage Example's resume mentions HTML and CSS; the JD asks for Kubernetes. “制作静态页面。” → BAD: “使用 Kubernetes 构建云原生前端平台。” → Safer: “使用 HTML、CSS 制作静态页面。” Ask about relevant infrastructure work separately; do not insert an unverified skill.
8. DO NOT: invented metrics. Quinn Example at Example Pebble Works: “整理客户问题并更新帮助文档。” → BAD: “将客户满意度提升 35%，每月节省 200 小时。” → Safer: “整理客户问题，更新对应帮助文档。” Ask for verified resolution or usage evidence if quantification matters.
9. DO NOT: unit and period drift. Sky Example: “试运行两周，错误率从 4% 降至 3%。” → BAD: “全年错误率下降 25 个百分点。” → Safer: “在两周试运行中将错误率从 4% 降至 3%。” Retain the source values and period.
