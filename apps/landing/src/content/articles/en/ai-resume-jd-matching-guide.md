## The short answer

Matching a resume to a job description is not copying every phrase from the posting. It is mapping the role's signals to work you have actually done, then showing the evidence. A dependable sequence is: extract signals, build an evidence map, rewrite one bullet, and review it line by line.

### 1. Extract three kinds of signals

Split the job description into:

- **Responsibilities**: what you will deliver, such as building frontend observability or running growth experiments.
- **Capabilities**: the skills and methods needed, such as React, SQL, or A/B testing.
- **Constraints**: location, seniority, language, and other screening requirements.

Responsibilities determine which experience comes first. Capabilities guide keyword coverage. Constraints should be explicit near the top when they matter. A tool should never invent a skill you have not used.

### 2. Replace adjectives with evidence

“Owned performance improvements” is hard to verify. “Reduced first-load time from 3.2 seconds to 1.1 seconds through code splitting and caching” carries evidence. Use this check for each important bullet:

> action + object + method + result (a number or an observable change)

When you do not have a number, use scope, scale, time span, or before/after change. Keeping the factual boundary is better than asking an AI to guess an impressive metric.

### 3. Use AI for comparison, not fabrication

A trustworthy AI resume workflow shows the matched job-description passage, the proposed edit, the reason, and the requirements that remain uncovered. You accept the change only after checking it. That improves ATS readability without replacing your voice or facts.

The [Magic Resume JD matching tool](/en/tools/jd-matcher) places job signals beside resume sections. Try the synthetic example first, then apply the verified changes to your own resume and read the exported PDF once more.

### Final checklist

1. The target role at the top matches the role you are applying for.
2. Important responsibilities appear in your most relevant recent work.
3. Every keyword has evidence in a real experience or project.
4. No company, number, or skill was added without confirmation.
5. The exported PDF has sensible text order, headings, and dates.

The goal of JD matching is to make your evidence easier to understand—not to make a resume look machine-written.
