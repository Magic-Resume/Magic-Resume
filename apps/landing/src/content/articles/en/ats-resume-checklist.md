## The short answer

An ATS mostly reads the text and structure of a resume. It does not approve a document because it “looks ATS-friendly.” Before applying, verify that keywords are backed by facts, headings are unambiguous, dates are consistent, the PDF text can be copied, and important information is not trapped in an image.

### Eight quick checks

1. **File format**: export a PDF unless the employer explicitly asks for DOCX.
2. **Text order**: copy the full PDF into a plain-text editor and make sure name, experience, and education remain in the right order.
3. **Clear headings**: use familiar labels such as Experience, Education, and Skills instead of relying on icons alone.
4. **Consistent dates**: use one month/year format and reverse chronological order.
5. **Evidence-backed keywords**: place skills in projects, responsibilities, or outcomes instead of pasting a list of fashionable terms.
6. **Readable contact details**: email, phone, and links should be real text, not a background image.
7. **Focused length**: remove old, irrelevant details so the strongest evidence has room.
8. **Human review**: check spelling, links, numbers, and company names. Automation can reduce repetition; it cannot take responsibility for your facts.

The [Magic Resume template guide](/en/tools/resume-templates) gives you a sensible starting layout for different roles. A template is only the frame—the evidence and its order do the matching work.

If a template looks fine in a browser but copies into a scrambled text order, do not submit it yet. Fix the structure before tuning color or decoration.
